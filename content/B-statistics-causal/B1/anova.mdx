You have $k$ groups and a continuous outcome. The two-sample t-test handles $k=2$;
for $k > 2$, running all pairwise t-tests inflates the Type I error (the
multiple-testing problem addressed in the next lesson). **One-way ANOVA** tests
whether any group mean differs from the others in a single test.

## The model

Group $j$ ($j = 1, \ldots, k$) contains $n_j$ observations. The model is:

$$
X_{ij} = \mu_j + \varepsilon_{ij}, \quad \varepsilon_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2),
$$

all errors independent with equal variance (homoscedasticity). The grand mean is
$\bar X = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{j,i} X_{ij}$ with $N = \sum_j n_j$.

**Null:** $H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2 = \cdots = \mu_k$ (all group means equal).

**Alternative:** at least one pair differs.

## Variance decomposition

Total variability is partitioned into two pieces:

$$
\underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^k \sum_{i=1}^{n_j} (X_{ij} - \bar X)^2}_{\text{SST}} =
\underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^k n_j (\bar X_j - \bar X)^2}_{\text{SSB}} +
\underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^k \sum_{i=1}^{n_j} (X_{ij} - \bar X_j)^2}_{\text{SSW}}.
$$
<FN n="1" />

- **SSB** (sum of squares between groups): variation explained by group membership.
- **SSW** (sum of squares within groups, or error): unexplained variation within groups.

**Degrees of freedom:** $\mathrm{df}_B = k-1$, $\mathrm{df}_W = N-k$.

**Mean squares:**

$$
\mathrm{MSB} = \frac{\mathrm{SSB}}{k-1}, \qquad \mathrm{MSW} = \frac{\mathrm{SSW}}{N-k}.
$$

## The F-statistic

Under $H_0$, both MSB and MSW estimate $\sigma^2$, and their ratio follows an
$F$ distribution:

$$
F = \frac{\mathrm{MSB}}{\mathrm{MSW}} \sim F_{k-1,\, N-k} \text{ under } H_0.
$$
<FN n="2" />

Under $H_1$, $\mathrm{MSB}$ is inflated (it captures real group differences), so
large $F$ values provide evidence against $H_0$.

**Connection to the t-test.** For $k=2$, the $F$ statistic equals $T^2$ from the
two-sample t-test, and $F_{1, N-2} = t_{N-2}^2$. ANOVA is a generalization of the
t-test, not a separate tool.

## Assumptions

1. **Independence** of observations (by design).
2. **Normality** of residuals (robust for large $n$ by CLT).
3. **Homoscedasticity** (equal variances). Welch's ANOVA relaxes this.

Violations of (3) can inflate Type I error. Check with Levene's test or plot group
variances.

## Post-hoc tests

ANOVA's rejection of $H_0$ says some means differ; it does not say which ones.
Post-hoc pairwise comparisons (Tukey's HSD, Bonferroni-corrected t-tests) identify
the specific pairs, subject to a family-wise error rate.

## Implementation

```python-static
import numpy as np
from scipy import stats

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)

# Three groups with different means
groups = [rng.normal(5.0, 1.5, 30),
          rng.normal(6.2, 1.5, 30),
          rng.normal(5.5, 1.5, 30)]
N = sum(len(g) for g in groups)
k = len(groups)

grand_mean = np.concatenate(groups).mean()
ssb = sum(len(g) * (g.mean() - grand_mean)**2 for g in groups)
ssw = sum(((g - g.mean())**2).sum() for g in groups)
msb = ssb / (k - 1)
msw = ssw / (N - k)
F   = msb / msw
p   = 1 - stats.f.cdf(F, k-1, N-k)
print(f"SSB={ssb:.2f}, SSW={ssw:.2f}")
print(f"F({k-1}, {N-k}) = {F:.4f}, p = {p:.4f}")

# Verify with scipy
F_sp, p_sp = stats.f_oneway(*groups)
print(f"scipy F={F_sp:.4f}, p={p_sp:.4f}")
```

The F-statistic and p-value from manual computation and `scipy.stats.f_oneway`
agree to floating-point precision.

Running many ANOVA tests or following up with many pairwise comparisons reintroduces
the problem that ANOVA was designed to avoid; the next lesson addresses that with
formal multiple-testing corrections.
