You have $k$ groups and a continuous outcome. The two-sample t-test handles $k=2$;
for $k > 2$, running all pairwise t-tests inflates the Type I error (the
multiple-testing problem addressed in the next lesson). **One-way ANOVA** tests
whether any group mean differs from the others in a single test.

## The model

Group $j$ ($j = 1, \ldots, k$) contains $n_j$ observations. The model is:

$$
X_{ij} = \mu_j + \varepsilon_{ij}, \quad \varepsilon_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2),
$$

all errors independent with equal variance (homoscedasticity). The grand mean is
$\bar X = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{j,i} X_{ij}$ with $N = \sum_j n_j$.

**Null:** $H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2 = \cdots = \mu_k$ (all group means equal).

**Alternative:** at least one pair differs.

## Variance decomposition

Total variability is partitioned into two pieces:

$$
\underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^k \sum_{i=1}^{n_j} (X_{ij} - \bar X)^2}_{\text{SST}} =
\underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^k n_j (\bar X_j - \bar X)^2}_{\text{SSB}} +
\underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^k \sum_{i=1}^{n_j} (X_{ij} - \bar X_j)^2}_{\text{SSW}}.
$$
<FN n="1" />

- **SSB** (sum of squares between groups): variation explained by group membership.
- **SSW** (sum of squares within groups, or error): unexplained variation within groups.

**Degrees of freedom:** $\mathrm{df}_B = k-1$, $\mathrm{df}_W = N-k$.

**Mean squares:**

$$
\mathrm{MSB} = \frac{\mathrm{SSB}}{k-1}, \qquad \mathrm{MSW} = \frac{\mathrm{SSW}}{N-k}.
$$

## The F-statistic

Under $H_0$, both MSB and MSW estimate $\sigma^2$, and their ratio follows an
$F$ distribution:

$$
F = \frac{\mathrm{MSB}}{\mathrm{MSW}} \sim F_{k-1,\, N-k} \text{ under } H_0.
$$
<FN n="2" />

Under $H_1$, $\mathrm{MSB}$ is inflated (it captures real group differences), so
large $F$ values provide evidence against $H_0$.

$F$ is a signal-to-noise ratio, and the cleanest way to feel its scale is to hold
the noise fixed and vary only the separation between group means. Take the three
groups in the code block below: means near 4.8, 6.6, and 5.8 with common spread
$\sigma = 1.5$. They give $\mathrm{SSW} = 182.3$ and $F = 11.56$. Now collapse the
means so all three groups share the same target while keeping the identical
within-group noise (the same draws): $\mathrm{SSW}$ is unchanged at $182.3$, but
$\mathrm{SSB}$ falls from $48.5$ to $5.8$, and $F$ drops to $1.37$. Under $H_0$ both
$\mathrm{MSB}$ and $\mathrm{MSW}$ estimate $\sigma^2$, so $F$ hovers near 1; the
jump to 11.56 is the between-group signal showing through the same noise floor.

**Connection to the t-test.** For $k=2$, the $F$ statistic equals $T^2$ from the
two-sample t-test, and $F_{1, N-2} = t_{N-2}^2$. ANOVA is a generalization of the
t-test, not a separate tool.

## Assumptions

1. **Independence** of observations (by design).
2. **Normality** of residuals (robust for large $n$ by CLT).
3. **Homoscedasticity** (equal variances). Welch's ANOVA relaxes this.

Violations of (3) can inflate Type I error. Check with Levene's test or plot group
variances.

## Post-hoc tests

ANOVA's rejection of $H_0$ says some means differ; it does not say which ones.
Post-hoc pairwise comparisons (Tukey's HSD, Bonferroni-corrected t-tests) identify
the specific pairs, subject to a family-wise error rate.

## Implementation

```python-static
import numpy as np
from scipy import stats

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)

# Three groups with different means
groups = [rng.normal(5.0, 1.5, 30),
          rng.normal(6.2, 1.5, 30),
          rng.normal(5.5, 1.5, 30)]
N = sum(len(g) for g in groups)
k = len(groups)

grand_mean = np.concatenate(groups).mean()
ssb = sum(len(g) * (g.mean() - grand_mean)**2 for g in groups)
ssw = sum(((g - g.mean())**2).sum() for g in groups)
msb = ssb / (k - 1)
msw = ssw / (N - k)
F   = msb / msw
p   = 1 - stats.f.cdf(F, k-1, N-k)
print(f"SSB={ssb:.2f}, SSW={ssw:.2f}")
print(f"F({k-1}, {N-k}) = {F:.4f}, p = {p:.4f}")

# Verify with scipy
F_sp, p_sp = stats.f_oneway(*groups)
print(f"scipy F={F_sp:.4f}, p={p_sp:.4f}")
```

The F-statistic and p-value from manual computation and `scipy.stats.f_oneway`
agree to floating-point precision.

## Exercises

**Exercise 1.** Three groups of $n = 5$ each have means
$\bar X_1 = 10, \bar X_2 = 12, \bar X_3 = 14$ and a grand mean $\bar X = 12$.
Compute SSB and its degrees of freedom $\mathrm{df}_B$, then the mean square
MSB.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Step 1.** Apply $\mathrm{SSB} = \sum_j n_j(\bar X_j - \bar X)^2$ with $n_j = 5$:

$$
\mathrm{SSB} = 5(10-12)^2 + 5(12-12)^2 + 5(14-12)^2 = 5\cdot4 + 0 + 5\cdot4 = 40.
$$

**Step 2.** With $k = 3$ groups, $\mathrm{df}_B = k - 1 = 2$, so

$$
\mathrm{MSB} = \frac{\mathrm{SSB}}{k-1} = \frac{40}{2} = 20.
$$

</details>

**Exercise 2.** Prove the variance partition $\mathrm{SST} = \mathrm{SSB} + \mathrm{SSW}$
by expanding $(X_{ij} - \bar X) = (X_{ij} - \bar X_j) + (\bar X_j - \bar X)$ and
showing the cross-term vanishes.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Step 1.** Write the total deviation as a sum of a within-group and a
between-group piece, then square:

$$
(X_{ij} - \bar X)^2 = (X_{ij} - \bar X_j)^2 + 2(X_{ij} - \bar X_j)(\bar X_j - \bar X) + (\bar X_j - \bar X)^2.
$$

**Step 2.** Sum over $i$ within group $j$. The cross-term factors because
$(\bar X_j - \bar X)$ is constant in $i$:

$$
2(\bar X_j - \bar X)\sum_{i=1}^{n_j}(X_{ij} - \bar X_j) = 2(\bar X_j - \bar X)\cdot 0 = 0,
$$

since $\sum_i (X_{ij} - \bar X_j) = 0$ by definition of the group mean.

**Step 3.** What remains after summing over both $i$ and $j$ is

$$
\sum_{j,i}(X_{ij} - \bar X)^2 = \underbrace{\sum_{j,i}(X_{ij} - \bar X_j)^2}_{\mathrm{SSW}} + \underbrace{\sum_j n_j(\bar X_j - \bar X)^2}_{\mathrm{SSB}},
$$

where the last sum collapsed $\sum_i$ of a constant to a factor $n_j$. This is the
partition.

</details>

**Exercise 3 (code).** Implement the ANOVA sums of squares in numpy (no scipy)
and confirm the identity $\mathrm{SST} = \mathrm{SSB} + \mathrm{SSW}$ holds on
random data, then report the F-statistic.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

```python
import numpy as np

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
groups = [rng.normal(5.0, 1.5, 30),
          rng.normal(6.2, 1.5, 30),
          rng.normal(5.5, 1.5, 30)]

allx = np.concatenate(groups)
N, k = allx.size, len(groups)
grand = allx.mean()

sst = ((allx - grand) ** 2).sum()
ssb = sum(g.size * (g.mean() - grand) ** 2 for g in groups)
ssw = sum(((g - g.mean()) ** 2).sum() for g in groups)

F = (ssb / (k - 1)) / (ssw / (N - k))
print("SST:", round(sst, 4), " SSB+SSW:", round(ssb + ssw, 4))
print("F:", round(F, 4))
assert np.isclose(sst, ssb + ssw)
```

The total sum of squares equals the between-group plus within-group sums to
floating-point precision, the identity proved in Exercise 2.

</details>

the problem that ANOVA was designed to avoid; the next lesson addresses that with
formal multiple-testing corrections.
