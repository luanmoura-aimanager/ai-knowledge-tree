Schools differ in average test scores. Estimating each school's mean from its own
students alone is noisy for small schools. Using the grand mean for all schools
ignores real differences. **Hierarchical (multilevel) models** find the middle ground:
each school has its own parameter, but those parameters are drawn from a common
population-level distribution. This allows small groups to borrow strength from the
rest.

## Three estimators and the pooling spectrum

Suppose $J$ groups, group $j$ has $n_j$ observations $Y_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(\theta_j, \sigma^2)$.

- **No pooling:** estimate each $\theta_j$ independently as $\bar Y_j$. Unbiased but
  high variance for small $n_j$.
- **Complete pooling:** use the grand mean $\bar Y$ for all groups. Low variance but
  ignores between-group differences.
- **Partial pooling:** the hierarchical estimator shrinks $\bar Y_j$ toward the
  grand mean, with the amount of shrinkage depending on $n_j$ and the between-group
  variance.

## The two-level model

**Level 1 (observation model):**

$$
Y_{ij} \mid \theta_j \sim \mathcal{N}(\theta_j, \sigma^2), \quad i = 1, \ldots, n_j.
$$

**Level 2 (prior on group parameters):**

$$
\theta_j \mid \mu, \tau^2 \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \tau^2), \quad j = 1, \ldots, J.
$$

**Hyperprior (optional):** $p(\mu, \tau^2)$ over the hyperparameters.

The joint distribution is:

$$
p(\{Y_{ij}\}, \{\theta_j\}, \mu, \tau^2) = p(\mu, \tau^2) \prod_j p(\theta_j \mid \mu, \tau^2) \prod_i p(Y_{ij} \mid \theta_j).
$$

## Posterior of $\theta_j$

When $\mu$ and $\tau^2$ are known, the conjugate Gaussian update gives:

$$
\theta_j \mid \{Y_{ij}\}, \mu, \tau^2 \sim \mathcal{N}(\hat\theta_j, V_j),
$$

where:

$$
V_j = \left(\frac{1}{\tau^2} + \frac{n_j}{\sigma^2}\right)^{-1}, \qquad
\hat\theta_j = V_j\left(\frac{\mu}{\tau^2} + \frac{n_j \bar Y_j}{\sigma^2}\right).
$$

Rewriting $\hat\theta_j$ as a weighted average:

$$
\hat\theta_j = (1 - \lambda_j)\,\bar Y_j + \lambda_j\,\mu,
\quad \lambda_j = \frac{\sigma^2/n_j}{\sigma^2/n_j + \tau^2}.
$$

The shrinkage weight $\lambda_j$ is large (shrink toward $\mu$) when the group is
small ($n_j$ small) or the between-group variance $\tau^2$ is large relative to
within-group variance. When $n_j \to \infty$, $\lambda_j \to 0$ and the estimate
is the group mean.

## Implementation

```python-static
import numpy as np
from scipy import stats

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)

J = 8
mu_true, tau_true, sigma_true = 50.0, 10.0, 15.0
ns = rng.integers(5, 40, J)  # unequal group sizes

theta_true = rng.normal(mu_true, tau_true, J)
data = [rng.normal(theta_true[j], sigma_true, ns[j]) for j in range(J)]
group_means = np.array([d.mean() for d in data])

# Plug-in hyperparameters (in practice estimated by EM or MCMC)
grand_mean = np.concatenate(data).mean()
tau2_hat   = max(group_means.var() - sigma_true**2 * np.mean(1/ns), 0.1)
sigma2     = sigma_true**2

# Partial-pooling estimates
lam = sigma2 / ns / (sigma2 / ns + tau2_hat)
partial = (1 - lam) * group_means + lam * grand_mean

print(f"{'j':>3} {'n':>4} {'truth':>8} {'no-pool':>10} {'partial':>10}")
for j in range(J):
    print(f"{j:3d} {ns[j]:4d} {theta_true[j]:8.2f} {group_means[j]:10.2f} {partial[j]:10.2f}")

rmse_np = np.sqrt(((group_means - theta_true)**2).mean())
rmse_pp = np.sqrt(((partial - theta_true)**2).mean())
print(f"\nRMSE no-pooling={rmse_np:.2f}, partial-pooling={rmse_pp:.2f}")
```

Partial pooling reduces RMSE because small groups are shrunk toward a more accurate
estimate while large groups retain their own data.
