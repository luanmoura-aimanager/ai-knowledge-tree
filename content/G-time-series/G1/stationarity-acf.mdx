A forecasting model has to assume that the future resembles the past in some
stable way. If the mean drifts and the variance grows without bound, no finite
sample tells you anything about tomorrow. **Stationarity** is the precise version
of "the statistical structure does not move," and it is the assumption almost
every classical time-series method rests on.

## Weak stationarity

A time series is a sequence of random variables $\{X_t\}$ indexed by integer time
$t$. It is **weakly stationary** (or second-order stationary) when three things
hold for all $t$:

1. The mean is constant: $\mathbb{E}[X_t] = \mu$.
2. The variance is finite: $\operatorname{Var}(X_t) = \sigma^2 < \infty$.
3. The covariance between two times depends only on their gap, not on where the
   gap sits: $\operatorname{Cov}(X_t, X_{t+h})$ depends on $h$ alone.

The third condition is the substantive one. It says the relationship between a
point and its neighbour $h$ steps away is the same in 1990 as in 2020.

## The autocovariance function

Define the **autocovariance function** at lag $h$:

$$
\gamma(h) = \operatorname{Cov}(X_t, X_{t+h}) = \mathbb{E}\big[(X_t - \mu)(X_{t+h} - \mu)\big].
$$

Under stationarity this is a function of $h$ only. Two properties fall straight
out of the definition.

**It is even.** Covariance is symmetric, so

$$
\gamma(-h) = \operatorname{Cov}(X_t, X_{t-h}) = \operatorname{Cov}(X_{t-h}, X_t) = \gamma(h),
$$

where the last step relabels $t-h$ as the base time and uses stationarity.

**At lag zero it is the variance.** Setting $h=0$ gives $\gamma(0) = \operatorname{Var}(X_t) = \sigma^2$.

## The autocorrelation function (ACF)

Dividing by the variance removes the units and bounds the quantity:

$$
\rho(h) = \frac{\gamma(h)}{\gamma(0)}.
$$

This is the **autocorrelation function**. By Cauchy-Schwarz, $|\gamma(h)| \le \gamma(0)$,
so $\rho(h) \in [-1, 1]$ with $\rho(0) = 1$. The ACF answers: how strongly is a
value correlated with the one $h$ steps before it?

## The partial autocorrelation function (PACF)

The ACF at lag 2 is inflated by an indirect path: $X_t$ correlates with $X_{t-1}$,
which correlates with $X_{t-2}$, so $X_t$ and $X_{t-2}$ share correlation routed
through $X_{t-1}$. The **partial** autocorrelation at lag $h$ strips that out. It
is the correlation between $X_t$ and $X_{t-h}$ after regressing both on the
intervening values $X_{t-1}, \dots, X_{t-h+1}$:

$$
\alpha(h) = \operatorname{Corr}\big(X_t - \hat{X}_t,\; X_{t-h} - \hat{X}_{t-h}\big),
$$

where $\hat{X}_t$ is the linear projection of $X_t$ on the intervening lags. The
PACF is the coefficient on $X_{t-h}$ in the best linear predictor of $X_t$ from
the last $h$ lags.

## Reading the order off the plots

The two functions cut off in opposite ways, which is what makes them a
diagnostic pair:

- An **AR(p)** process has a PACF that is zero beyond lag $p$ (the predictor needs
  exactly $p$ lags) and an ACF that decays gradually.
- An **MA(q)** process has an ACF that is zero beyond lag $q$ and a PACF that
  decays gradually.

"Where does it cut off" reads the order; "which one cuts off" reads the family.
The AR/MA lesson derives why.

## Estimating from data

Given a sample $x_1, \dots, x_n$, the sample autocovariance uses the single mean
$\bar{x}$ and divides by $n$ (not $n-h$), which keeps the estimated $\hat{\gamma}$
a valid positive-semidefinite covariance function:

$$
\hat{\gamma}(h) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{t=1}^{n-h} (x_t - \bar{x})(x_{t+h} - \bar{x}).
$$

```python
import numpy as np

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)

# Simulate an AR(1): X_t = 0.7 X_{t-1} + e_t  (stationary since |0.7| < 1)
n, phi = 4000, 0.7
e = rng.normal(0, 1, n)
x = np.zeros(n)
for t in range(1, n):
    x[t] = phi * x[t-1] + e[t]

def acf(x, max_lag):
    x = x - x.mean()
    g0 = (x * x).mean()
    return np.array([(x[:n-h] * x[h:]).mean() / g0 for h in range(max_lag + 1)])

r = acf(x, 5)
print("sample ACF:", np.round(r, 3))
# Theory for AR(1): rho(h) = phi**h
print("theory phi^h:", np.round(phi ** np.arange(6), 3))
```

The sample ACF tracks $\phi^h$, the geometric decay an AR(1) predicts, confirming
both the stationarity of $\phi=0.7$ and the gradual ACF that flags an AR process.
