A slowly decaying ACF looks like a strong AR process, but it can also be the
signature of a series that is not stationary at all. The borderline case is the
**unit root**, and telling the two apart matters: differencing a stationary series
introduces problems, while modelling a unit-root series as stationary gives
spurious results. Formal tests resolve the ambiguity.

## The random walk and the unit root

Consider the AR(1) process $X_t = \phi X_{t-1} + \varepsilon_t$ with white noise
$\varepsilon_t$. The previous lesson required $|\phi| < 1$ for stationarity. At
exactly $\phi = 1$ this becomes the **random walk** $X_t = X_{t-1} + \varepsilon_t$.

Unrolling the recursion from $X_0 = 0$:

$$
X_t = \sum_{s=1}^{t} \varepsilon_s, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(X_t) = t\,\sigma^2.
$$

The variance grows linearly with $t$, so condition 2 of stationarity fails. The
value $\phi = 1$ is the root of the characteristic equation $1 - \phi z = 0$ at
$z = 1$, hence the name **unit root**. A series with a unit root is made
stationary by differencing once: $\Delta X_t = X_t - X_{t-1} = \varepsilon_t$ is
white noise. Such a series is called integrated of order one, $I(1)$.

## The augmented Dickey-Fuller test

The ADF test asks whether $\phi = 1$<FN n="1" />. Subtract $X_{t-1}$ from both sides of the
AR(1):

$$
\Delta X_t = (\phi - 1) X_{t-1} + \varepsilon_t = \delta X_{t-1} + \varepsilon_t,
\qquad \delta = \phi - 1.
$$

Now $\phi = 1$ becomes $\delta = 0$. The test regresses $\Delta X_t$ on $X_{t-1}$
and checks whether the coefficient $\delta$ is zero.

- **Null hypothesis** $H_0: \delta = 0$ (unit root present, non-stationary).
- **Alternative** $H_1: \delta < 0$ (so $\phi < 1$, stationary).

The "augmented" part adds lagged differences $\Delta X_{t-1}, \dots, \Delta X_{t-p}$
to the regression to soak up any short-run autocorrelation in $\varepsilon_t$, so
the residual is genuinely white:

$$
\Delta X_t = \delta X_{t-1} + \sum_{i=1}^{p} c_i \, \Delta X_{t-i} + \varepsilon_t.
$$

The test statistic is the usual $t$-ratio $\hat{\delta} / \operatorname{se}(\hat{\delta})$.
The catch: under the null, $X_{t-1}$ is itself non-stationary, so this ratio does
not follow a $t$ distribution. Its critical values come from the **Dickey-Fuller
distribution**, which is more negative than the normal (the 5% critical value is
about $-2.86$, not $-1.65$)<FN n="3" />. A statistic below the critical value rejects the unit
root.

## KPSS: the complementary test

The ADF and KPSS tests swap which case is the null<FN n="2" />:

| Test | $H_0$ | $H_1$ |
|---|---|---|
| ADF | unit root (non-stationary) | stationary |
| KPSS | stationary | unit root |

KPSS models the series as a deterministic level plus a random walk plus noise,
and tests whether the random-walk variance is zero. Running both removes the
asymmetry of a single test, where failing to reject the null is weak evidence.
The four outcomes:

- ADF rejects, KPSS does not reject: **stationary** (both agree).
- ADF does not reject, KPSS rejects: **unit root** (both agree).
- Both reject: contradictory, often a sign of a different structure.
- Neither rejects: not enough information; the data is uninformative.

## Demonstration

```python
import numpy as np

rng = np.random.default_rng(3)
n = 600

# (a) random walk: has a unit root.  (b) stationary AR(1) with phi=0.5
rw  = np.cumsum(rng.normal(0, 1, n))
ar1 = np.zeros(n)
for t in range(1, n):
    ar1[t] = 0.5 * ar1[t-1] + rng.normal(0, 1)

def adf_delta_tstat(x, p=1):
    dx = np.diff(x)
    # regressors: X_{t-1} and p lagged differences, plus intercept
    rows = []
    y = []
    for t in range(p, len(dx)):
        feats = [1.0, x[t]]                      # x[t] is X_{t-1} for dx[t]
        feats += [dx[t-i] for i in range(1, p+1)]
        rows.append(feats); y.append(dx[t])
    X = np.array(rows); y = np.array(y)
    beta, *_ = np.linalg.lstsq(X, y, rcond=None)
    resid = y - X @ beta
    dof = len(y) - X.shape[1]
    s2 = (resid @ resid) / dof
    cov = s2 * np.linalg.inv(X.T @ X)
    se_delta = np.sqrt(cov[1, 1])               # coefficient on X_{t-1}
    return beta[1] / se_delta                    # the ADF t-statistic

print(f"ADF stat, random walk:   {adf_delta_tstat(rw):.2f}  (near 0: do NOT reject unit root)")
print(f"ADF stat, AR(1) phi=0.5: {adf_delta_tstat(ar1):.2f}  (very negative: reject unit root)")
print("5% Dickey-Fuller critical value with intercept: -2.86")
```

The random walk gives a statistic close to zero (fails to reject the unit root),
while the stationary AR(1) gives a strongly negative statistic (rejects it),
matching the Dickey-Fuller critical value of $-2.86$.
