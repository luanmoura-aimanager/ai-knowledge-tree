The ACF and PACF diagnostics from the first lesson point at two model families.
This lesson builds them. The **autoregressive** (AR) model says today is a linear
function of recent values; the **moving-average** (MA) model says today is a
linear function of recent shocks. Almost every classical forecast is some
combination of the two.

## White noise: the building block

A **white-noise** process $\{\varepsilon_t\}$ has mean zero, constant variance
$\sigma^2$, and zero autocovariance at every nonzero lag: $\gamma_\varepsilon(h) = 0$
for $h \neq 0$. It carries no predictable structure; everything below is built by
filtering white noise.

## The MA(q) process

A moving-average process of order $q$ is a weighted sum of the last $q$ shocks<FN n="1" />:

$$
X_t = \varepsilon_t + \theta_1 \varepsilon_{t-1} + \dots + \theta_q \varepsilon_{t-q}.
$$

Its autocovariance is finite-range, which is the whole point.

**Derivation of the MA(1) autocovariance.** Take $X_t = \varepsilon_t + \theta \varepsilon_{t-1}$.

Step 1, variance:

$$
\gamma(0) = \operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_t + \theta \varepsilon_{t-1}) = \sigma^2 + \theta^2 \sigma^2 = (1 + \theta^2)\sigma^2,
$$

using independence of the two shocks.

Step 2, lag 1:

$$
\gamma(1) = \mathbb{E}[(\varepsilon_t + \theta \varepsilon_{t-1})(\varepsilon_{t-1} + \theta \varepsilon_{t-2})] = \theta \, \mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_{t-1}^2] = \theta \sigma^2,
$$

since only the $\varepsilon_{t-1}$ terms overlap.

Step 3, lag 2 and beyond: $X_t$ and $X_{t-2}$ share no common shock, so
$\gamma(h) = 0$ for $h \ge 2$. The ACF is

$$
\rho(1) = \frac{\theta}{1 + \theta^2}, \qquad \rho(h) = 0 \text{ for } h \ge 2.
$$

The ACF cuts off after lag $q$. That is the MA signature.

## The AR(p) process

An autoregressive process of order $p$ regresses the current value on its own past:

$$
X_t = \phi_1 X_{t-1} + \dots + \phi_p X_{t-p} + \varepsilon_t.
$$

**The Yule-Walker equations.**<FN n="2" /> Multiply the AR(p) equation by $X_{t-h}$ for $h > 0$
and take expectations. Since $\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_t X_{t-h}] = 0$ (the future
shock is uncorrelated with the past),

$$
\gamma(h) = \phi_1 \gamma(h-1) + \dots + \phi_p \gamma(h-p), \qquad h > 0.
$$

The autocovariance satisfies the same recursion as the process itself. Dividing by
$\gamma(0)$ gives the ACF recursion. For AR(1) this yields $\rho(h) = \phi^h$, the
geometric decay seen in the first lesson: the ACF tails off rather than cutting
off, which is the AR signature.

## Stationarity and invertibility through the characteristic roots

Write the AR(p) with the lag operator $L$, defined by $L X_t = X_{t-1}$:

$$
\phi(L) X_t = \varepsilon_t, \qquad \phi(L) = 1 - \phi_1 L - \dots - \phi_p L^p.
$$

**Stationarity condition.** The AR is stationary when every root of the polynomial
$\phi(z) = 0$ lies *outside* the unit circle, $|z| > 1$. For AR(1), $1 - \phi z = 0$
gives $z = 1/\phi$, so $|1/\phi| > 1$ means $|\phi| < 1$, recovering the earlier
condition. A root on the circle ($|z| = 1$) is the unit root of the previous
lesson.

**Invertibility condition.** Symmetrically, an MA(q) is **invertible** when the
roots of $\theta(z) = 1 + \theta_1 z + \dots + \theta_q z^q$ lie outside the unit
circle. Invertibility lets you rewrite the MA as an infinite AR, so the unobserved
shocks can be recovered from observed past values. Without it, two different MA
parameters give the identical ACF and the model is not identified.

## Duality

The two families are mirror images. A stationary AR(p) equals an MA($\infty$)
(invert $\phi(L)$); an invertible MA(q) equals an AR($\infty$). This duality is why
the ACF/PACF pair identifies them: where one cuts off, the other tails off.

```python
import numpy as np

rng = np.random.default_rng(7)
n = 200_000

# AR(1), phi=0.6: ACF should tail off as phi^h
phi = 0.6
ar = np.zeros(n)
for t in range(1, n):
    ar[t] = phi * ar[t-1] + rng.normal()

# MA(1), theta=0.8: ACF should cut off after lag 1
theta = 0.8
e = rng.normal(size=n)
ma = e[1:] + theta * e[:-1]

def acf(x, H):
    x = x - x.mean(); g0 = (x*x).mean()
    return np.array([(x[:len(x)-h]*x[h:]).mean()/g0 for h in range(H+1)])

print("AR(1) ACF:", np.round(acf(ar, 3), 3), " theory phi^h:", np.round(phi**np.arange(4), 3))
r = acf(ma, 3)
print("MA(1) ACF:", np.round(r, 3), " theory rho(1)=theta/(1+theta^2):", round(theta/(1+theta**2), 3))
```

The AR(1) ACF decays as $\phi^h$ while the MA(1) ACF matches $\theta/(1+\theta^2)$
at lag 1 and vanishes after, the exact cut-off-versus-tail-off contrast that reads
model family off the plots.
