Labeled image datasets are expensive; unlabeled images are essentially
free. **Self-supervised pretraining** turns this asymmetry into model
quality: pretrain a vision Transformer<FN n="2" /> on hundreds of millions of
unlabeled images, then fine-tune on a small labeled set. Two methods
dominate: **DINO** (student-teacher distillation, no labels needed) and
**MAE** (masked image modeling, BERT for pixels). The D5 SSL track
covers the general theory; this lesson is the vision-specific story.

## Why SSL for vision

Supervised ImageNet pretraining was the standard for years. Its limits:

- 1.3M images is a small dataset by foundation-model standards.
- Many real-world tasks have a domain gap from ImageNet.
- ImageNet labels are noisy (~10% errors at the class level).

Self-supervised pretraining on much larger unlabeled corpora (LAION,
internal image dumps) consistently beats supervised pretraining on
downstream transfer, while needing no labels at all.

## DINO

Caron et al. (2021), Facebook AI Research.

**Setup**: two networks, the **student** $g_\theta$ and the **teacher**
$g_\xi$, both vision Transformers. They receive different augmented
crops of the same image and must produce similar features. The teacher's
parameters are an **exponential moving average** of the student's; this
prevents collapse without negative pairs.

Loss: cross-entropy between the student's and teacher's softmaxed
features, where each feature vector is interpreted as a probability
distribution. **Centering** of the teacher's features (subtracting a
running mean) and **temperature sharpening** keep the representations
informative.

DINO produces remarkable emergent properties:

- The pretrained **[CLS] token attention** highlights the object of
  interest, even though the model was never told what the object is.
- **k-NN classification** on DINO features matches supervised
  ImageNet classification at 78% accuracy without any labels.
- Linear probes on top of frozen DINO features approach supervised
  fine-tuning accuracy.

**DINOv2** (2023) scaled this to a curated 142M-image dataset and
produced general-purpose visual features used as backbones for
classification, segmentation, depth, and 3D tasks. Closest vision has
to a general-purpose feature extractor.

## MAE (Masked Autoencoders)

He et al. (2022). The image analogue of BERT's masked language modeling<FN n="1" />.

**Setup**: mask 75% of the image patches at random. An **asymmetric
encoder-decoder** ViT:

- The **encoder** sees only the unmasked 25% of patches (much cheaper
  than processing all patches).
- The **decoder** (small, ~8 layers) receives the encoder's output plus
  learned mask tokens at masked positions and predicts the original
  pixel values.

Loss: mean squared error on pixel values of the masked patches.

Why it works:

- The 75% mask rate forces the model to learn high-level semantic
  features (low-level texture is not enough to fill in 75% of an
  image).
- The asymmetric encoder is efficient: it processes a quarter of the
  patches in the forward pass, making 1.6B-parameter MAEs trainable
  with modest compute.

After pretraining, throw away the decoder and use the encoder as a
backbone. Fine-tuning matches or beats DINO and supervised
pretraining on classification and detection.

## SimCLR / MoCo / iBOT (background)

Other SSL methods used in vision before DINO and MAE:

- **SimCLR** (2020): contrastive learning with augmented pairs (similar
  views close, others apart). Needs huge batches and many negatives.
- **MoCo** (2020): contrastive with a memory bank of negatives; works
  at smaller batches.
- **BYOL** (2020): student-teacher without negatives, similar to DINO.
- **iBOT** (2022): combines DINO-style distillation with masked-image
  prediction for the best of both.

The D5 SSL track covers the family broadly; here the focus is on the
practical vision-specific recipe.

## Practical recipe

For a typical vision project:

1. **Start with DINOv2 or MAE pretrained on a large corpus**. Freeze
   the backbone, train a small task head. Excellent for fast
   experimentation.
2. **Fine-tune the whole backbone** for your task when you have enough
   data and compute. Improves performance.
3. **Train SSL from scratch on your domain** only if the domain is far
   from natural images (medical, satellite, microscopy) and you have
   millions of images.

For most teams, option 1 is what works. DINOv2 weights, MAE weights,
and CLIP image-encoder weights are all reasonable starting points.

## The big picture

Modern vision moved from "supervised ImageNet pretraining" to
"self-supervised pretraining on much larger unlabeled corpora", with
fine-tuning on smaller labeled task datasets. SSL methods are now the
default upstream training; supervised fine-tuning is downstream.
