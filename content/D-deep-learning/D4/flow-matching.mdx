DDPM-style discrete diffusion was the breakthrough; **flow matching** is
its cleaner continuous-time successor. Both train a generator by
matching a noise-removing field; flow matching has a simpler objective
and runs at fewer sampling steps for the same quality. **Consistency
models** push the same generative quality into one or two steps. SD 3,
FLUX, and many 2024+ models use these methods as the default.

## Continuous-time view of diffusion

DDPM is a discretization of a continuous-time stochastic differential
equation:

$$
dx_t = f(x_t, t)\, dt + g(t)\, dW_t,
$$

where $W$ is Wiener noise. The reverse SDE (or its equivalent ODE) has
the form

$$
dx_t = [f(x_t, t) - g(t)^2\, \nabla \log p_t(x_t)]\, dt + g(t)\, dW_t.
$$

Sampling from data is integrating this reverse-time equation; learning
the **score** $\nabla \log p_t$ is what diffusion does.

The continuous-time view unified DDPM, NCSN, and other score-based
methods; flow matching is the next conceptual simplification.

## Flow matching

Lipman et al. (2023). Skip the noise/score framing<FN n="1" />. Define a
**probability path** $p_t(x)$ that interpolates between a simple base
$p_0$ (e.g., $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$) and the data distribution $p_1$. Each
path has an associated **vector field** $v_t(x)$ that transports
samples along it: a point at time $t$ at position $x$ moves with
velocity $v_t(x)$.

**Train a neural network $v_\theta(x, t)$ to match the true vector field.**

For a Gaussian path (the simplest choice),

$$
x_t = (1 - t)\, x_0 + t\, x_1, \qquad x_0 \sim p_0,\ x_1 \sim p_{\text{data}},
$$

the conditional vector field is $u_t(x \mid x_1) = x_1 - x_0$. The
flow-matching loss is

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{FM}} = \mathbb{E}_{t, x_0, x_1}\!\left[\lVert v_\theta((1-t)x_0 + t x_1,\ t) - (x_1 - x_0)\rVert^2\right].
$$

Compare to DDPM's loss: same shape (predict a target given a noisy
input), simpler target (a straight-line direction instead of a noise
sample at a specific schedule).

## Rectified flow

Liu et al. (2023). The same idea, with iterative **rectification**<FN n="2" />:
after training a flow $v_\theta$, sample many pairs $(x_0, x_1)$ from
the flow by integrating $v_\theta$, then retrain a new flow on these
pairs. Each rectification makes the trajectories straighter, reducing
the number of sampling steps needed.

A fully rectified flow can sample in 1-2 steps with comparable quality
to a 50-step DDPM.

## Why flow matching often wins

- **Simpler training objective**: no noise schedules, no
  parameterization choices ($v$, $\epsilon$, $x_0$).
- **Straighter paths**: a Gaussian interpolation produces less curved
  trajectories than the DDPM noising process, so fewer sampling steps
  are needed.
- **Cleaner conditioning**: classifier-free guidance maps
  straightforwardly.
- **Better empirical scaling**: matches or beats DDPM-style training
  at the same parameter count.

SD 3 switched from DDPM to flow matching. FLUX, Pyramid Flow, and
many 2024 models followed.

## Consistency models

Song et al. (2023). Train a model that directly maps any noisy
sample at any timestep to the same final clean sample<FN n="3" />:

$$
f_\theta(x_t, t) = f_\theta(x_{t'}, t')\ \ \text{for all }t, t'.
$$

After training, sampling is one network call: $\hat x_0 = f_\theta(x_T, T)$.

Two training routes:

- **Consistency distillation**: start from a pretrained diffusion model,
  distill it into a consistency model.
- **Consistency training**: train from scratch with a loss that enforces
  the consistency property between adjacent timesteps.

Consistency models have similar quality to many-step diffusion at 1-2
steps. Combined with adversarial losses, they're approaching
GAN-level inference speed with diffusion-level quality.

## What's next

The generative-modeling landscape as of 2024:

- **Flow matching** is the dominant training framework for new models.
- **Consistency distillation** is the dominant deployment technique
  for fast inference.
- **DiTs** are replacing U-Nets in the backbone.
- **Multimodal extensions** (video, 3D, audio) are the active research
  frontier.

Less than a decade after diffusion's debut, the field has converged on
a clean recipe: train a continuous-time flow with a Transformer backbone,
distill into a few-step sampler, condition with cross-attention on a
strong multimodal text encoder. The next subsection (D5) returns to a
different problem: pretraining representations without labels.

## Exercises

**Exercise 1.** For the straight-line Gaussian path $x_t = (1 - t)x_0 + t x_1$ with
endpoints $x_0 \sim p_0$, $x_1 \sim p_{\text{data}}$, derive the conditional vector
field $u_t(x \mid x_0, x_1)$ that transports a sample along the path, and confirm it
is the flow-matching target $x_1 - x_0$.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Step 1.** The vector field is the velocity of the path: the time derivative of
position along the interpolation, holding the endpoints fixed.

**Step 2.** Differentiate $x_t = (1 - t)x_0 + t x_1$ with respect to $t$:

$$
\frac{dx_t}{dt} = -x_0 + x_1 = x_1 - x_0.
$$

**Step 3.** This derivative is constant in $t$: along a straight line the velocity
does not change. So $u_t(x \mid x_0, x_1) = x_1 - x_0$, the target the
flow-matching loss regresses $v_\theta((1-t)x_0 + t x_1,\ t)$ onto. The straightness
of the path is exactly why the target is a single direction rather than a
schedule-dependent quantity.

</details>

**Exercise 2.** Compare the flow-matching loss to the DDPM loss
$\mathbb{E}\lVert \epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)\rVert^2$. Identify what plays
the role of the regression target in each, and explain why the lesson calls the
flow-matching target "simpler".

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Step 1.** DDPM regresses the network onto the noise sample $\epsilon$ that was
added to form $x_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon$.
The target is a Gaussian draw whose relationship to $x_t$ is mediated by the
schedule coefficients $\bar\alpha_t$. The mapping from $(x_0, x_t, t)$ to the target
depends on the chosen noise schedule.

**Step 2.** Flow matching regresses the network onto $x_1 - x_0$, a straight-line
direction between a base sample and a data sample. There is no schedule: the target
is the difference of two endpoints, identical for every $t$ along a given path.

**Step 3.** "Simpler" means the target carries no schedule and no parameterization
choice (predict $\epsilon$ vs $x_0$ vs $v$). A single interpolation defines both
the noisy input and the target velocity, so there are fewer design knobs and the
paths are straight, which also reduces the number of integration steps needed at
sampling time.

</details>

**Exercise 3.** A consistency model enforces $f_\theta(x_t, t) = f_\theta(x_{t'}, t')$
for all $t, t'$ on the same trajectory, so one network call maps any noisy point to
the clean sample. Explain why this property permits one-step sampling, and what is
given up relative to a many-step diffusion sampler.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Step 1.** Diffusion sampling integrates the reverse process in many small steps
because each step only removes a little noise; the clean sample emerges after the
whole trajectory. A consistency model is trained so that evaluating $f_\theta$ at
any point on a trajectory returns the same endpoint $\hat x_0$.

**Step 2.** Given that property, sampling needs no integration: draw $x_T \sim
\mathcal{N}(0, I)$ and evaluate $\hat x_0 = f_\theta(x_T, T)$ once. The consistency
constraint has folded the entire reverse trajectory into a single function
evaluation.

**Step 3.** What is given up is fidelity per call. The many-step sampler corrects
its trajectory at each step, so errors do not compound into one jump; the one-step
map must approximate the whole trajectory with a single network, which is harder and
typically slightly lower quality unless combined with a few refinement steps or
adversarial losses. Consistency models trade some sample quality for one-or-two-step
inference speed.

</details>

## The big picture for D4

D4 walked the generative tree from autoencoders (no proper density,
useful encoder) through VAEs (variational density), GANs (adversarial
samples, no density), normalizing flows (exact density, restricted
architecture), diffusion (high quality, many steps), latent diffusion
(production-grade), to flow matching and consistency models (the
modern cleanup).

The thread that ties it all together: **score-based / flow-based
methods that learn to invert a noising or smoothing process**. The
specific algorithmic choice (DDPM, score matching, flow matching,
consistency) is converging on what works best at scale; the underlying
generative recipe is now stable enough to be a foundational tool
across modalities.
